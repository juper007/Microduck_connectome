## Normative interpretation

The [P8-V2 final protocol](P8-V2-FINAL-PROTOCOL.md) freezes 20 Hz RGB, 50 Hz neural/control and a full 1,000 ms **after arm** for P8-03, with a continuous pre-arm visual baseline and a body already moving. The [local-reference protocol](P8-03-LOCAL-REFERENCE-PROTOCOL-V1.md) requires a durable arm marker before scored arm, fresh positive applied motion and health at arm, and terminal accounting for any armed/unknown-arm attempt. It does not require synchronous graph priming or worker creation inside the scored second. Therefore pre-arm priming and READY workers are faithful only if the actual arm follows a fresh movement/health/state check, the durable marker precedes releasing scored work, no pre-arm neural/control output is counted, and motion refresh remains continuous during preparation. A pre-arm stop or fault is retained and fails safe; it cannot be hidden by shifting arm.

The normative control target is **50 Hz**. The R1 scorer's `len(control_publish) >= 40` first appeared with the harness and has no documented protocol tolerance. For the timing probe, require 50 actual distinct control decisions/publications, one in each of 50 intended 20 ms periods. Healthy neutral outputs may be deliberately suppressed at robotd transport for stop causality, so control publication is separate from `robot.move` ACK continuity. A separate scorer revision for future behavioral execution requires independent science/architecture review; the timing probe never treats 40 as 50.

The scored interval is the 1,000 ms after the durable arm event. Probe slots are `k=0..49` with deadlines `arm_ns + 20,000,000*k`. A real neural graph call and a real control publication must complete in their assigned slot before `arm_ns + 1,000,000,000`; a pre-arm prime, a tick at the exact end boundary, a duplicate timestamp, a counted missed deadline, and a synthetic filler are ineligible. Keep the existing inclusive legacy scorer boundary for historical R1 reproduction; the timing probe's slot accounting is a new, explicit diagnostic, not a reinterpretation of behavioral evidence.

## R1 root causes and feasibility limit

R1 starts the arm clock before synchronous perception/neural prime and worker startup; its first control publish is 37–64 ms after arm. The scheduler then skips 4–17 neural deadlines per ID. Full neural-call p50/p95/p99 is 13.3/24.1/28.5 ms (85/405 above 20 ms), but the archive does not split graph compute, Python scheduling/GIL wait, arbiter-lock wait, and trace bookkeeping. Neural and `robot.move` share an arbiter lock for stop/move ordering; this safety ordering must remain intact unless separately proven equivalent. The motion worker serially performs move ACK, post-ACK state wait, and body read before refreshing again, producing only 32–43 ACKs. R1 move RPC p95 is 6.2 ms, so RPC alone does not explain the cadence.

P3's 1.065 ms p95 graph benchmark used a synthetic 570-node/21,142-edge graph on x86. The actual P8 graph has 1,098 nodes/72,782 edges on Thor under concurrent simulator load. P3 does not establish feasibility or infeasibility. The R1 evidence rules out an unsupported **C — INFEASIBLE_UNDER_FROZEN_CONTRACT** claim. A/B remains to be resolved by the reviewed real-stack probe.

## Causal remediation selected for first probe

1. Perform a real pre-arm perception/neural warmup, retaining its timestamps and any stop/fault. Start neural, control and motion workers to a bounded READY barrier before arm. Keep acknowledged move refresh active through preparation; revalidate fresh positive applied vx, moving pose, robotd health, policy and safety immediately before the durable marker and release. If readiness or freshness fails, safe-stop before arm with no timing result.
2. Release workers against one captured monotonic `arm_ns`, with absolute 20 ms deadlines. Preserve real skipped-cycle counters and fail the probe on any missing slot. Do not catch up with duplicated work or backfilled timestamps. Record scheduled deadline, wake, start, completion and thread identity for every neural/control/motion period.
3. Keep the stop arbiter and robotd motor authority. Put only `robot.move` command/ACK in the 50 Hz motion critical path. Read robotd state and body pose asynchronously, retaining raw receive/request/response timestamps, requested/applied twist, `limited_by`, policy, safety, body speed and TTL/deadman observations. Join these streams by timestamp in the probe scorer without dropping the frozen observation evidence requirement. A stale state/pose, queue overflow or observer failure fails closed.
4. Add diagnostic-only monotonic spans around latest-frame acquisition, arbiter-lock wait/hold, sensory mapping, external-input construction, graph runtime step, readout, decoder/safety/latch, trace/ledger bookkeeping, and control publish. Record wall and thread CPU time where supported; their difference is only an estimate of descheduling/GIL contention. Do not alter biological or behavioral values, graph, decoder, safety envelope or command limits. Record queue depth, coalescence/drop count, scheduled versus actual periods and process CPU usage where practical.

This first remediation addresses the observed late start and serial motion observation directly; it instruments the unresolved neural overrun rather than assuming a cause. If graph or lock-held computation still consumes the 20 ms budget on Thor, stop after the failed probe and return to an architecture decision. No rate/count/window weakening is allowed.

The scheduler uses a latest-frame cache, not a blocking neural input queue. The probe therefore records the cache access span and source age; its `perception_wait_ns` is zero by construction. The outer arbiter-lock wait is measured in the probe scheduler, because the inner neural callback runs under that reentrant lock. The inner span is retained as a diagnostic cross-check and must not be interpreted as the full lock wait.

At release, the probe checks a recent 200 ms pose-derived moving interval, current applied state, allowed policy, non-fallen/non-limp safety state, health, and motion ACK freshness. Its arm callback sets the geometry anchor to the latest independently observed moving pose and rephases 20 Hz visual cadence to the actual arm instant; the genuine pre-arm prime remains retained and uncounted. The offline scorer independently reconstructs the recent pose speeds and every scored frame's raw body packet, pose-relative virtual center, rendered fractional RGB hash, distance, bearing and image area. It also checks frozen synthetic ToF identity and values, neural event-to-ledger equality, and each authentic neutral control intent. An interrupted final simulator down cannot pass.

Every run must satisfy all of the following within its own `[arm, arm+1,000 ms)` diagnostic interval. Failure of any run makes the probe FAIL, with no behavioral matrix:

- Durable marker precedes arm; all workers READY, motion/health/freshness valid at arm; 1,000 ms is not shortened or extended.
- Exactly 50 **real valid neural graph calls** and 50 **real control publications** occupy all distinct slots `k=0..49`, with no missed deadline, duplicate/filler count, invalid/stale input, or unexplained gap. Each has a recorded deadline, wake/start, completion, source frame and sequence. Neural/control maximum inter-event gap remains no greater than the frozen 40 ms gap rule; slot completeness is the stronger probe requirement.
- Exactly 20 valid visual frames at frozen 20 Hz, with original source/pose/RGB lineage and no gap above the frozen 100 ms rule.
- Exactly 50 intended 20 ms motion refresh periods with positive `robot.move` ACK and zero post-stop positive moves. Maximum ACK gap no greater than 100 ms. At least the frozen 40 distinct fresh applied-state and pose observations with valid moving-body evidence, no stale state/pose, no deadman/TTL expiry and no body-motion stop at retained observations.
- Zero scheduler exceptions, queue overflow, unexplained drop/coalescence, safety-limit violations, or contamination. Official final down and port/socket probe PASS.

Report every run's count, first/last and maximum gap per stream; p50/p95/p99 total neural call, graph compute-only, mapping, decoder, lock wait, perception, publish, move RPC and logging/serialization spans; p95 wake jitter, maximum overrun, cumulative drift, missed neural/control/motion periods, queue depth/drops, process CPU and thread CPU where available.
